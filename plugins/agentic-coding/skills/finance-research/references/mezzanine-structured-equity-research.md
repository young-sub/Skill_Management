# Mezzanine / structured equity research workflow

Use when the user asks whether PE/private credit should invest via CB, BW, EB, CPS, RCPS, preferred equity, convertible note, PIPE, rescue financing, or other downside-protected structures.

## Core lens

Do not screen only by theme exposure. Rank candidates by:

1. **Funding need / deal access** — does the issuer actually need structured capital, or can it raise cheaper public debt/equity?
2. **Base-case repayment capacity** — EBITDA, operating cash flow, cash balance, working-capital burden, debt maturity, backlog conversion.
3. **Downside protection** — collateral, seniority, covenants, put/redemption, liquidation preference, anti-dilution, registration rights, board/info rights.
4. **Equity upside** — conversion premium, warrant coverage, valuation, catalysts, probability of conversion.
5. **Negotiability** — large high-quality issuers usually offer poor PE terms; smaller or cash-burning issuers allow structure but need stricter protection.

## Preferred output shape

Start with a clear investability ranking:

- **High priority**: names where a PE/private credit structure is realistic and the risk can be protected.
- **Conditional**: interesting exposure but structure/valuation/risk makes terms critical.
- **Avoid / public-market only**: good company but too large, too cheap financing access, or weak thematic purity.

For each candidate, include:

- Why it is/is not suitable for mezzanine
- Key current financial anchors: market cap, revenue, operating income/loss, net income/loss, cash, debt, leverage, cash burn if relevant
- What security fits best: CB, BW, EB, CPS/RCPS, senior secured convertible note, convertible preferred, rescue financing, PIPE
- Downside protection required
- Main deal-breakers / DD questions

## Structure design heuristics

### Profitable small/mid-cap listed issuer

Typical fit: domestic CB/BW, sometimes redeemable preferred.

- Size: keep dilution and market liquidity manageable; often 2-8% of market cap unless rescue financing.
- Coupon/YTM: coupon 2-5%, YTM 6-10% depending on collateral and credit quality.
- Conversion premium: 10-25% above reference price.
- Reset: allow limited downward reset, but use a hard floor (often 75-85% of initial conversion price) to avoid uncontrolled dilution.
- Put: investor put after 18-24 months.
- Call: restrict early issuer call; if allowed, require make-whole or minimum IRR.
- Protection: negative pledge, debt incurrence limit, related-party restrictions, additional CB/BW issuance limits, change-of-control put.

### Cash-burning growth issuer

Typical fit: senior secured convertible note or convertible preferred with warrants and milestone tranches.

- Coupon: cash + PIK structure, often 8-12% total return before equity upside.
- OID: 2-5% where risk is high.
- Warrants: 25-75% coverage depending on cash burn and valuation.
- Tranches: initial draw plus milestone draws tied to signed orders, deliveries, gross margin, regulatory approvals, or cash burn limits.
- Protection: first/second lien on IP, inventory, receivables, equipment; cash burn covenant; minimum cash; registration rights; anti-dilution; board observer.
- Redemption/change-of-control: require greater of stated multiple (e.g. 1.25x-1.5x) or minimum IRR.

### Large high-quality public issuer

Often **not** a good PE mezzanine target despite strong business quality.

- These issuers can usually raise cheaper public debt/equity and resist PE-friendly terms.
- Treat as opportunistic only: market dislocation, acquisition financing, refinancing, litigation/regulatory overhang, or liquidity event.
- Structure tends to be low-coupon convertible preferred or second-lien convertible note with fewer covenants.

### Private growth company / pre-IPO

Typical fit: RCPS/convertible preferred.

- Liquidation preference: 1.0x-1.5x, participating only if risk is unusually high.
- Dividend: 6-8% cumulative PIK if downside protection is needed.
- Redemption: after 5 years, subject to legal solvency limits.
- Anti-dilution: broad-based weighted average.
- Protective provisions: board seat/observer, budget approval, debt cap, M&A/asset-sale consent, information rights.
- Tranching: tie follow-on funding to ARR, gross margin, production yield, regulatory approval, customer concentration improvement, or IPO readiness.

## DD checklist

- Existing CB/BW/preferred terms, reset floors, call options, warrants, and dilution overhang
- Security package: receivables, inventory, equipment, IP, real estate, listed shares, guarantees
- Senior debt documents and restricted payment/debt-incurrence baskets
- Backlog quality, cancellation rights, customer concentration, government-contract assignment limits
- Working-capital cycle, cash conversion, receivable aging, inventory obsolescence
- Cash burn, runway, financing gap, capex needs
- Insider ownership, pledge status, governance, related-party transactions
- Sector-specific regulation: export control, defense security, ITAR/CFIUS, procurement qualification, listing rules
- Registration/resale rights for listed PIPE or convertible deals

## Drone/UAV sector example from prior work

For drone-related names, the investability ranking differed from pure thematic exposure:

- **Most realistic Korean listed mezzanine candidate: Firstec / 퍼스텍** — profitable, mid-small market cap, defense/unmanned exposure, potential working-capital need. Candidate structure: KRW 15-25bn secured CB/BW, 3-year maturity, coupon 3-4%, YTM 7-9%, 10% conversion premium, 80-85% reset floor, investor put after 18-24 months, debt/additional-CB/change-of-control protections.
- **High-risk US pure-play: Red Cat (RCAT)** — high drone purity but loss-making and cash-burning. Candidate structure: USD 50-100m senior secured convertible note or convertible preferred PIPE, 6-8% cash coupon + 2-4% PIK, 3-5% OID, 15-25% conversion premium, 25-50% warrant coverage, milestone tranches, cash burn covenant, collateral over receivables/inventory/IP, registration rights.
- **Quality but hard-to-access: AeroVironment (AVAV)** — strong drone/loitering-munition exposure and scale, but larger public issuer with better financing access. Candidate structure only opportunistic: USD 150-300m convertible preferred or second-lien convertible after M&A/refinancing/dislocation, 5-7% coupon, 25-35% conversion premium, soft-call restrictions.
- **Large defense/tech primes** (LMT, NOC, RTX, Hanwha Aerospace, LIG, KAI, Hanwha Systems, Alphabet, Amazon): business quality can be high, but PE mezzanine fit is usually poor because funding access is cheap and drone exposure is diluted.
- **Drone distributors** (e.g. JC Hyun, PC Direct): downside may be protected with inventory/receivables, but thematic exposure is weak; treat as secured credit, not pure drone growth.

## Pitfalls

- Do not recommend the highest-growth thematic company if downside protection is not credible.
- Do not assume CB = protected downside. A deeply out-of-the-money unsecured CB in a cash-burning company is equity risk with coupon optics.
- Avoid over-indexing on market cap alone; liquidity, existing convertibles, pledge status, and collateral matter.
- For public-company data, state whether figures come from filings/market-data tools and the date basis. For Korean data, note whether figures are consolidated/separate and whether they are annual or quarterly.
